HUMANITARIAN OVERVIEW1 
People in the Far North continue to be severely affected by the floods. According to the Regional Delegation of Health, 
68,285 households, approximately 409,710 people, including 139,651 children under 5 and 29,428 pregnant women, 
were affected by the floods, as of 11 October 2024.2

Some 56,084 houses were destroyed or damaged, 262 schools flooded, 65 health centres affected, and 82,509 of 
farmland flooded.3 After the rains, the rising river levels are causing concerns about a worsening situation in the coming 
days and weeks. The progressive rise in groundwater levels and the river overflowing continue in the Logone et Chari 
division, increasing the risk of river flooding. At the beginning of October, people affected by floods in Logone et Chari 
started to displace to areas considered flood secure. Some 9,747 people (1,527 households) were registered in host 
families and spontaneous sites in Kousseri between 2 and 9 October,4 with an additional 4,054 people (576 households) 
reported in different locations in Logone et Chari between 16 and 21 October.5 
The Government, local authorities/elites, and a variety of humanitarian organizations are providing assistance to those 
affected.6 
UNICEF’S RESPONSE 
 
Health 
• 
130,015 people vaccinated against cholera, including 22,500 children aged 1 
to 4 years in the localities of Yagoua, Vélé and Maga, Mayo-Danay. 99.57 per 
cent of the targeted 130,572 people were reached during this preventive 
campaign. 
• 
2 cholera management kits were prepositioned, one in the Far North and one in 
the North region, to anticipate a potential outbreak of the disease. 
• 
12,353 patients received health services with UNICEF’s support, in the Yagoua, 
Kaikai, Vele, (Mayo-Danay), Mada and Blagoua (Logone-et-Chari) health districts. 
• 
Provision of supplies for disease management, including 3,000 boxes of 
paracetamol, 2,500 boxes of oral rehydration salts, 1,000 rapid diagnostic tests 
for malaria, 10 rapid diagnostic tests for cholera, medication for malaria treatment 
and other diseases, and 500 boxes of medical gloves in the most affected divisions 
(Mayo-Danay and Logone-et-Chari).

WASH 
• 
500 WASH kits distributed in the IDPs sites in Kousseri on 24 October. 
• 
Support provided for the construction of 10 emergency latrines for 100 
households in IDPs sites in Palia, Maga, (4) and in Madagascar, Kousseri (6). 
Identification of other locations for the construction of emergency latrines in 
Kousseri IDP sites is ongoing. 
• 
Identification and diagnosis of non-functional water points in areas affected by 
flooding in Kousseri, Maga and Yagoua ongoing. 
• 
Previously, 1,430 WASH kits were distributed in Vele and Yagoua (Mayo-Danay) 
and in Mayo-Sangué (Mayo-Tsanaga). In addition, 734 packs of 300 tablets of 
aquatabs each were distributed to households in Maga (Mayo-Danay). 
Distributions were accompanied by key messages on good hygiene practices. 
 
 
Education 
• 
560 WASH and dignity kits7 and 10 educational kits8 were distributed to schools 
in Blangoua, Darak, Kousséri, Logone-Birni, Makary, Waza, and Zina. 
• 
19 tents were installed in Yagoua, Maga, (Mayo-Danay), Zina and Makary 
(Logon-et-Chari) to provide safe space for education and child protection activities. 
 
 
Child 
Protection 
• 
1,019 children affected by the floods received mental health and psychosocial 
support through mobile child-friendly spaces and sport activities in Maga (MayoDanay) and in Logone Birni and Fotokol (Logone-et-Chari), 406 children during 
the week of 14 October. 
• 
34 children received case management, including family counselling on 
managing children with special needs. 
• 
A rapid assessment of the needs of children affected by the floods was carried 
out in all the subdivisions of Mayo-Danay and Logone-et-Chari. The report is being 
finalized. 
 
 
Nutrition 
• 
15,050 cartons of Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food (RUTF) and 500 cartons of 
therapeutic milk were prepositioned in various hospitals and warehouses in the 
Mayo-Danay and Logone-et-Chari divisions. 
• 
2,961 SAM children were admitted to the programmes and were successfully 
treated in August and September in health districts affected by the floods (Bogo, 
Mada, Maga, Vele, Yagoua (Mayo-Danay), Fotokol, Goulfey, Kousseri, Makary 
(Logone-et-Chari)).
